WHO sets no health-based value for pH. 6.5 to 8.5 is the range usually used as optimum for drinking water, and very low or very high pH can irritate skin and eyes. These bands are adjustable settings, not legal limits. pH is only one test: canal water is not safe to drink untreated even at a normal pH, because bacteria and chemicals do not show up in pH.

Microplastics are plastic particles smaller than 5 mm. The boat filters a water sample and a camera counts the particles it can see, and this is a simulation. Very small particles need a microscope or lab analysis, and a lab must confirm that a particle is plastic. WHO (2019) found insufficient evidence of a health risk at current levels and asked for more research. Its priority is removing pathogens and chemicals. The levels shown only compare your own samples, because there is no official limit and sampling methods differ between studies.
